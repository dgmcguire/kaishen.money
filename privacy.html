Privacy

Privacy policy

Effective October 17, 2026.

Kaishen is a budgeting app operated by Dan McGuire. This page describes what it handles when you connect a bank or brokerage. Questions and deletion requests go to privacy@kaishen.money.

What the app collects

When you connect an institution, Kaishen receives:

  • The institution name
  • Account names, types, masks, and balances
  • Transactions, including merchant, date, and amount
  • Investment holdings and securities
  • Liability details, including mortgages and other loans
  • The token Plaid uses so Kaishen can refresh that connection

Kaishen does not collect your bank or brokerage username or password. You sign in at the institution.

Why it is collected

Kaishen uses that data to show spending, balances, holdings, debts, and net worth for the accounts you chose to connect. It does not move money. It does not sell consumer data. It does not share that data with advertisers.

Where it is stored

Connection data and the token that refreshes a connection are stored on the server that runs Kaishen, encrypted at rest. The token stays on that server and is not put in the browser. Traffic to the server is HTTPS. Access to production is limited to the operator and requires multi-factor authentication.

Plaid

Account linking is provided by Plaid. Plaid’s own notice for people connecting an account is the Plaid end-user privacy policy.

How long it is kept

Kaishen keeps a connection’s data so it can keep showing those accounts. That data is deleted when you revoke access at the institution, when you revoke it through Plaid, or when the connection is removed. Email privacy@kaishen.money to ask for a connection to be removed. Backup copies are not kept longer than 30 days.

Changes

Kaishen posts changes to this policy on this page and updates the effective date.
